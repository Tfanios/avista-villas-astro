01 · Foundation

What Avista means

Avista is a small collection of three private houses in Sithonia, Halkidiki. One contemporary stone flagship and two warmer sister villas — kept by the people who built them, for guests who want a quiet week in Greece.
01
Essence

Three houses in the pines of Sithonia.

Between the forest and the Aegean sits a small, private collection of homes. Stone walls that hold the cool of the morning. Gardens that smell of olive leaf and pine. The closest thing most guests will find to a second home in Greece.

02
Promise

A quiet week, beautifully looked after.

We hand guests keys, a hand-drawn map of Sithonia, and a phone number that actually answers. The rest of the week belongs to them. Privacy is the deliverable. Hospitality is the foundation underneath it.

03
Personality

Warm, slow, deeply local.

We are the people behind Avista — a small team that grew up in Halkidiki and built these houses one by one. We know which beach is best on a Tuesday and where to find the fish that arrived at six in the morning. We also know how to leave guests to their holiday.

04
Audience

Families who chose Greece on purpose.

Parents who are done with big-resort holidays. Who want their children to spend a morning barefoot in a garden, swimming before breakfast, eating tomatoes that still taste of the sun. The kind of family who will remember the cicadas more than the check-in.

Pillar I

Privacy

Three houses, each set apart. Large gardens, tree-lines, space between neighbours. The view is always yours alone.

Pillar II

Place

Sithonia is a deliberate destination. Quieter than Mykonos. Greener than the Cyclades. Sea and forest within the same hour.

Pillar III

Personal hospitality

A small team you can reach directly. Real recommendations. A human presence when you want it and full privacy when you don't.

Two architectural styles

The collection has range, but it is not three random villas. Two architectural worlds, with the Private Resort standing alone, and the Villa and Superb Villa as warm sister properties.

Style I — The Flagship
Contemporary Stone

The Avista Private Resort. Modern handcrafted masonry, clean horizontal lines, a generous pergola and a pool with a waterfall feature. Designed in 2018, photographed in detail. The villa to lead with in marketing — the architectural piece de résistance of the collection.

Style II — The Sisters
Traditional Greek

The Avista Villa and the Avista Superb Villa. Same architectural language — terracotta floors, wood shutters, double-height ceilings, bougainvillea, olive grove. The Villa has a private pool; the Superb Villa does not. Together they offer two price points and two sizes inside the same warm Greek aesthetic.

03 · The Sithonia Guide

A guide for our guests

Sithonia is the quieter middle leg of Halkidiki. Forested, unhurried, closer to how Greece felt twenty years ago than how it looks on Instagram today. The Sithonia Guide is a major part of the website — and one of our strongest SEO assets, because nobody else writes about this peninsula in detail.

Why this section matters

Most luxury villa websites have one paragraph about the area. Ours has a full guide — and that's deliberate. Two reasons.

One: Guests booking a private villa in Greece are paying for *insider knowledge* as much as for the house itself. Anyone can rent a villa. We tell them which fishmonger to visit, which beach is empty on Tuesday, which baker opens at seven. That's the offer.

Two: SEO. People searching "best beaches Sithonia," "things to do Halkidiki," "where to eat Vourvourou" should land on our pages. Each guide page becomes an entry point to the brand.

01
Beaches

Our top eight, ranked honestly. Kavourotrypes, Goa, Karydi, Lagomandra, plus three our guests rarely find without us. Each with a one-paragraph note: when to go, what to bring, where to park, what the water is like.

02
Tavernas & restaurants

Five tavernas worth the drive — from the seafood shack in Sárti to the family taverna up the hill in Neos Marmaras. With approximate prices, what to order, whether to book, and the one menu item that actually defines the place.

03
Things to do

Beyond the beach. A boat trip from Vourvourou around the small islands. A quiet hike to Mount Itamos. Mount Athos seen from a boat (Athos itself is monks-only, but the southern coast is a different country in itself).

04
The local map

A hand-drawn map of Sithonia we share with every guest. It marks the beaches, tavernas, baker, fishmonger, wine shop, pharmacy, and the back road that everyone misses. Designed to print, fold, and keep in the glove box.

05
Practical Sithonia

Airport transfers, supermarket runs, pharmacies, the nearest hospital, where to buy good wine. The unromantic but essential page that takes the friction out of a first visit.

Voice for the guide pages

Specific. Honest. With opinions.

The Sithonia Guide is the place where the brand voice can have opinions. "We don't recommend Kavourotrypes after July 15 — too crowded, go to Lagomandra instead." "The taverna at the end of the road in Sárti looks like nothing from the outside; ignore the appearance, order the bream." This kind of specificity is what makes the guide useful and the brand believable. The Sithonia Guide reads like a friend texting you, not like a tourism board page.

06 · Logo

The mark, kept as is

The Avista wordmark — a handwritten script over the sub-label VILLAS — stays as it is. What follows is the system that surrounds it.
Avista
VILLAS
Primary lockup

White script on charcoal. Use for covers, primary brand surfaces, email signatures, and formal applications.

Clear space

Maintain minimum clear space equal to the height of the capital 'A' on all four sides.

Minimum size

Digital: 80px wide for the full lockup. Print: 20mm. Below this size, use the monogram (see Vol. II).

File formats

SVG for digital, PDF/EPS for print. Avoid PNG for any application that may scale.

Placement on photography

Let the image breathe

Avista
VILLAS

Always white when placed on photography. Always positioned in the calmest quadrant — sky, out-of-focus foliage, water. Keep clear of faces, doorways, and horizon lines.

07 · Photography

The property is the hero

Avista's photography should feel observed, almost unstaged. Natural light, a little shadow, room for imperfection. The frame should feel like a guest took it — with a very good eye.
01

Golden light

Shoot at the edges of the day — early morning and the hour before sunset. Midday light flattens stone and turns pool water into something clinical.

02

Slight desaturation

Pull saturation down 5–10% in edit. Greens should read olive, blues should read Aegean. This single edit is the biggest shift between stock photography and Avista photography.

03

Leave room in frame

Avoid centred compositions. Let sky, grass, stone, or pool occupy a third of the image. Crops should feel editorial, generous, unhurried.

04

People as suggestion

When families appear, show them from behind, as silhouettes, or as partial figures. Looking at the camera breaks the spell. Aspiration is stronger than pose.

05

Details over wides

Ten details outperform ten wide shots. Olive leaves backlit. Stone texture at close range. A breakfast table mid-meal. Instagram lives on details.

06

Each villa, its own register

The Private Resort photographs architecturally — clean, statement, contemporary. The Villa and the Superb Villa photograph warmly — bougainvillea, terracotta, lived-in light. Don't mix the registers.

08 · Voice

How Avista speaks

Fewer words, and the right ones. The Avista voice reads like the people who built and run the villas — warm, specific, local, unhurried.
The Avista voice

"We" — the people behind Avista.

The voice is collective, not individual. We use "we" and "our garden" because the houses belong to a small team that grew up in Halkidiki and built them. Warm, but without fuss. Specific — we know names of beaches, bakers, fishermen. Quietly confident.

We'd rather tell a guest the name of the best fishmonger than list amenities. We write the way we'd talk across a kitchen table — in shorter sentences than expected.
