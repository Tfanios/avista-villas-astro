01 · Foundation

What Avista means

Avista Villas is a small family collection of three private houses in Sithonia, Halkidiki. Each villa has its own character. Together, they share a single promise: quiet luxury, held in stone, pine, and olive.
01
Essence

Three houses in the pines of Sithonia.

Between the forest and the Aegean sits a small, private collection of homes. Stone walls that hold the cool of the morning. Gardens that smell of olive leaf and bougainvillea. The closest thing most guests will find to a second home in Greece.

02
Promise

Beautifully left alone.

Guests are handed keys, a hand-drawn map of Sithonia, and a phone number that actually answers. The rest of the week belongs to them. Privacy is the deliverable. Hospitality is the foundation underneath it.

03
Personality

Warm, slow, deeply local.

Avista speaks with the calm of a family friend who has lived in Halkidiki for a lifetime. Knows which beach is best on a Tuesday, where to find the fish that arrived at six in the morning, and how to leave guests to their holiday.

04
Audience

Families who chose Greece on purpose.

Parents who are done with big-resort holidays. Who want their children to spend a morning barefoot in a garden, swimming before breakfast, eating tomatoes that still taste of the sun. The kind of family who will remember the cicadas more than the check-in.

Pillar I

Privacy

Three houses, each set apart. Large gardens, tree-lines, space between neighbours. The view is always yours alone.

Pillar II

Place

Sithonia is a deliberate destination. Quieter than Mykonos. Greener than the Cyclades. Sea and forest within the same hour.

Pillar III

Personal hospitality

A founder-led team. Real recommendations. A human presence when you want it and full privacy when you don't.

02 · The Collection

Three houses, three moods

Each villa in the Avista collection has its own architectural story and its own feeling. The brand voice and visual system wrap around all three — but when speaking about a specific villa, lean into its particular character.
"A collection lets guests self-select. A family of six who wants space and a private pool chooses differently than a couple with a toddler who wants a short walk to the village. Position each villa for the guest who will love it most."

Naming rules for the collection

How to refer to each villa, consistently

Villa One
The Private Resort

Short name: Private Resort. Always leads with "The". Avoid "Villa 1" or "Resort Villa" in public copy.

Villa Two
The Garden Residence

Short name: The Garden. The word "residence" signals warmth and home. Avoid "Garden Villa" or "Villa 2".

Villa Three
The Cypress House

Short name: The Cypress. The name anchors the most photogenic element on the property. Avoid "Villa 3".

Suggested names — open to your revision. The important thing is that each villa has a proper name (a noun), rather than a number or type.

Primary lockup

The signature version: white script on charcoal. Use for covers, primary brand surfaces, email signatures, and formal applications.

Clear space

Maintain minimum clear space equal to the height of the capital 'A' on all four sides. Keep it uncrowded.

Minimum size

Digital: 80px wide for the full lockup. Print: 20mm. Below this size, consider a monogram or initial variant.

File formats

SVG for all digital surfaces. PDF or EPS for print. Avoid PNG for any application that may scale.

06 · Photography

The property is the hero

Avista's photography should feel observed, almost unstaged. Natural light, a little shadow, room for imperfection. The frame should feel like a guest took it — with a very good eye.
01

Golden light

Shoot at the edges of the day — early morning and the hour before sunset. Midday light flattens stone and turns pool water into something clinical.

02

Slight desaturation

Pull saturation down 5–10% in edit. Greens should read olive, blues should read Aegean. This single edit is the biggest shift between stock photography and Avista photography.

03

Leave room in frame

Avoid centred compositions. Let sky, grass, stone, or pool occupy a third of the image. Crops should feel editorial, generous, unhurried.

04

People as suggestion

When families appear, show them from behind, as silhouettes, or as partial figures. Looking at the camera breaks the spell. Aspiration is stronger than pose.

05

Details over wides

Ten details outperform ten wide shots. Olive leaves backlit. Stone texture at close range. A breakfast table mid-meal. Instagram lives on details.

06

Each villa, its own character

The Private Resort — architectural and clean. The Garden Residence — warm and lived-in. The Cypress House — atmospheric and dusky. Let each villa photograph in its own register.

The seasonal shot list

07 · Voice

How Avista speaks

Fewer words, and the right ones. The Avista voice reads like the person handing a guest the key on arrival — warm, specific, local, unhurried. Written in English, with the occasional Greek word where it earns its place.
The Avista voice

A host with a key in hand.

Picture a family friend who grew up in Halkidiki. Warm, but without fuss. Specific — they know names of beaches, bakers, fishermen. Quietly confident. They use "we" and "our garden" because it is theirs. They'd rather tell a guest the name of the best fishmonger than list amenities. They write the way they'd talk across a kitchen table: in shorter sentences than expected, occasionally in Greek when Greek is the right word.

08 · Instagram

The grid, as a rhythm

Instagram is where most guests will meet Avista first. The grid should read like a calm magazine spread: a steady rhythm of stone, green, water, interior, and a quiet typographic pause every nine posts.
"Where the pines end and the sea begins."

in stone & pine.
Grid system

A 9-tile rhythm

Every nine posts should contain the following mix. Plan in blocks of three, never one post at a time — that's how the grid stays cohesive.

6
Photography tiles

A mix across all three villas — exterior, interior, landscape, lifestyle detail. Edit with one consistent preset. Slight desaturation, warm highlights.

2
Quiet typographic tiles

Serif quote on cream, stone, or olive. One phrase per tile. Breathing room around the words. The Avista voice in pure form.

1
A charcoal pause

Once per rhythm, a dark tile — a short phrase in cream type. This is where the grid resets and the eye exhales.

To avoid

Carousel "quote graphics" with stock fonts. Oversaturated sunset drone shots. Lens flares and HDR. Promotional "20% off" tiles. Anything that feels built by a hotel marketing team rather than made by the family who runs the place.
